Cracking Ordered Clues: How to Solve "Earlier Than" and Exact-Gap Ladders

By Larry Henry | Published Nov 14, 2026 | Updated Nov 17, 2026 | 2135 words | min read

Clue five in Death on the Night Mail says “Mr. Quill was two compartments nearer the engine than Miss Ingram.” It doesn’t name a single compartment. Neither passenger is placed yet. So you shrug, move on to the next clue, and leave four free ✕s sitting on the table.

You weren’t wrong that the clue can’t place anyone by itself. You were wrong that it can’t mark anything. Compartments run in a fixed order, 1 to 4 from the engine back, and any clue that compares two positions on a fixed order rules out the ends of that order immediately.

Times (9:00 to 9:45 PM), compartments, floors (Ground to Third), and acts (Act I to Act IV) all work this way. Call them ladders. Suspects and weapons don’t have an order; ladders do, and every comparison on a ladder comes with hidden marks.

“Earlier Than” Clues: Three Free ✕s

The First and Last Rungs

Picture a ladder of four times: 9:00, 9:15, 9:30, 9:45. A clue says A was earlier than B. Before you know anything else:

  1. A can’t be on the last rung. If A was at 9:45, nobody could come after. ✕ at A / 9:45.
  2. B can’t be on the first rung. If B was at 9:00, nobody could come before. ✕ at B / 9:00.
  3. A and B can’t be the same person, if they’re items from different categories.

That third one is the mark you’ll miss. Clue 4 of Death at Ravenscroft Manor reads: “The guest holding the letter opener was seen earlier than the guest in the Library.” A letter opener is an Object, the Library is a Room, and Hobbs the butler saw each guest at one time. The person holding the letter opener can’t have been seen earlier than themselves, so the letter opener wasn’t in the Library.

The three marks land in three different blocks (X = ✕, O = ●, . = blank):

Time × Object                  Time × Room                  Object × Room
        Letter Opener                  Library                             Library
9:00         .                 9:00       X  <- B ≠ first   Letter Opener     X  <- A ≠ B
9:15         .                 9:15       .
9:30         .                 9:30       .
9:45         X  <- A ≠ last    9:45       .

On a hard grid, that Object × Room ✕ is often the only way to get information into that block early. Skip it and the bottom of the staircase stays empty for half the solve.

The Bounds Move When the Ladder Shrinks

An “earlier than” clue isn’t spent after its first three marks. Leave it uncrossed on your clue list.

Say another clue later rules B out of 9:45. B’s latest possible time is now 9:30, and since A is earlier than B, A’s latest possible time drops to 9:15. That’s a new ✕ for A at 9:30:

A earlier than B

Before                              After B loses 9:45
      9:00  9:15  9:30  9:45              9:00  9:15  9:30  9:45
A      .     .     .     X          A      .     .     X     X   <- new
B      X     .     .     .          B      X     .     .     X

It works from the bottom too: if A loses 9:00, B loses 9:15. Any ✕ at the edge of one item’s range can push the other item’s range inward. If you solve Kakuro, this is the same ceiling-and-floor squeeze from Kakuro Intersection Logic, with clock times standing in for digits.

Exact-Gap Clues: Count the Windows

A Gap of k Takes k Rungs Off Each End

Exact-gap clues fix the distance instead of just the direction. You’ll find them all over the browser cases:

  • Gap of 1: “one compartment nearer the engine” (Night Mail), “exactly fifteen minutes before” (Ravenscroft), “exactly one act before” (Curtain Call at the Gaslight Theatre)
  • Gap of 2: “two compartments nearer the engine” (Night Mail), “exactly half an hour before” on a 15-minute ladder (The Last Sitting at Hollowmere)

If B is exactly k rungs after A, then A can’t sit on the last k rungs and B can’t sit on the first k. That’s 2k ✕s on the first read, plus the A ≠ B mark when the two items come from different categories.

Before you mark anything, write the windows: the (A, B) position pairs that fit. There are always n − k of them on an n-rung ladder:

LadderGapValid windows (A, B)✕s for A✕s for B
4 rungs1(1,2) (2,3) (3,4)rung 4rung 1
4 rungs2(1,3) (2,4)rungs 3, 4rungs 1, 2
4 rungs3(1,4)rungs 2, 3, 4rungs 1, 2, 3
5 rungs1(1,2) (2,3) (3,4) (4,5)rung 5rung 1
5 rungs2(1,3) (2,4) (3,5)rungs 4, 5rungs 1, 2
5 rungs3(1,4) (2,5)rungs 3, 4, 5rungs 1, 2, 3

A gap of 3 on four rungs solves both items outright. A gap of 2 on four rungs crosses out half of each row.

A gap clue also mirrors itself. Rung i is possible for A only if rung i + k is possible for B. So every ✕ you place on A at rung i means a ✕ on B at rung i + k, and every ✕ on B at rung j means a ✕ on A at rung j − k. Keep gap clues live until both items are placed.

Collapsing the Window

The windows table pays off when another clue blocks one end of a window. Hollowmere’s arrival ladder has five rungs: 7:30, 7:45, 8:00, 8:15, 8:30 PM.

  • Clue 9: “The sitter mourning a mother arrived exactly half an hour before the sitter mourning a brother.” Gap of 2, so the windows are (7:30, 8:00), (7:45, 8:15), and (8:00, 8:30).
  • Clue 5: “Of the sitter who arrived at 8:30 PM and the sitter who arrived at 8:15 PM, one was mourning a father and the other was mourning a son.”

Clue 5 locks 8:15 and 8:30 to Father and Son, so the brother’s mourner didn’t arrive at either time. That kills two of the three windows. Here’s the Arrival × Mourning block with both clues marked:

            Son  Brother  Daughter  Mother  Father
7:30 PM      X      X        .        .       X
7:45 PM      X      X        .        .       X
8:00 PM      X      .        .        .       X
8:15 PM      .      X        X        X       .
8:30 PM      .      X        X        X       .

The Brother column has one open square. Brother = 8:00 PM, which leaves only the first window: Mother = 7:30 PM. Two ●s from two clues that never named a time for either relative.

Crossing Two Ladders at Once

Gaslight Theatre has two categories with a natural order: the act in which each actor first entered (Act I to Act IV) and the floor each one dressed on (Ground to Third). Every comparison clue in this case runs on the Entrance ladder. The floors still matter, because the Floor × Entrance block is the bridge that carries floor facts onto the act ladder.

Three clues meet on that bridge:

  • Clue 4: “The actor who dressed on the first floor first entered in Act IV.” That’s a ● in Floor × Entrance.
  • Clue 6: “Signor Valli first entered earlier in the play than the actor who dressed on the second floor.” Signor Valli ✕ Act IV, Second Floor ✕ Act I, and the third mark: Signor Valli ✕ Second Floor.
  • Clue 2: “Miss Lark first entered exactly one act before Mr. Bramwell.” Miss Lark ✕ Act IV, Mr. Bramwell ✕ Act I.

Mark the bridge block first:

Floor × Entrance
               Act I  Act II  Act III  Act IV
Ground Floor     .      .       .        X
First Floor      X      X       X        O     <- Clue 4
Second Floor     X      .       .        X     <- Clue 6 + auto-✕
Third Floor      .      .       .        X

The Second Floor row is down to Acts II and III. Now squeeze Clue 6’s ladder: Signor Valli entered earlier than the second-floor actor, and the second-floor actor entered no later than Act III. So Signor Valli entered no later than Act II.

Actor × Entrance
               Act I  Act II  Act III  Act IV
Mr. Bramwell     X      .       .        .     <- Clue 2
Miss Lark        .      .       .        X     <- Clue 2
Mrs. Penrose     .      .       .        .
Signor Valli     .      .       X        X     <- Clue 6, then the bridge

That Act III ✕ on Signor Valli came from a floor clue. No clue mentions Signor Valli and Act III together. Watch the Act IV column too: two ✕s already, and Transitive Cross-Referencing picks it up from here.

Practical Example: Cracking the Compartment Ladder in Death on the Night Mail

Back to the clue from the opening. This solves two passengers’ compartments without touching the luggage, and doesn’t give away the killer.

  • Compartments run 1 to 4 from the engine back, so “nearer the engine” means a lower number.
  • Clue 1: “The passenger in Compartment 3 was either Mr. Quill or Miss Ingram.”
  • Clue 5: “Mr. Quill was two compartments nearer the engine than Miss Ingram.”

Step 1: Write the windows. Gap of 2 on four rungs: (Quill 1, Ingram 3) or (Quill 2, Ingram 4).

Step 2: Mark the ends. Mr. Quill ✕ Compartments 3 and 4. Miss Ingram ✕ Compartments 1 and 2.

Step 3: Look down the Compartment 3 column. Clue 1 already crossed out Mrs. Fairweather and Captain Royle. Step 2 just crossed out Mr. Quill.

Passenger × Compartment
                    C1   C2   C3   C4
Mrs. Fairweather    .    .    X    .     <- Clue 1
Captain Royle       .    .    X    .     <- Clue 1
Miss Ingram         X    X    .    .     <- Clue 5
Mr. Quill           .    .    X    X     <- Clue 5

Step 4: Take the column single. Only Miss Ingram is left in Compartment 3, so she’s there. That forces the (1, 3) window and puts Mr. Quill in Compartment 1.

                    C1   C2   C3   C4
Mrs. Fairweather    X    .    X    .
Captain Royle       X    .    X    .
Miss Ingram         X    X    O    X
Mr. Quill           O    X    X    X

Half the block is solved from two clues, and Mrs. Fairweather and Captain Royle are now sharing Compartments 2 and 4. Clue 4 settles which is which.

Best Practices and Tips

Do:

  • Mark the A ≠ B ✕ whenever an ordered clue compares items from two different categories.
  • Keep “earlier than” and gap clues uncrossed until both items have a ● on the ladder.
  • Write out the two or three valid windows for a gap clue before you tap the grid.

Don’t:

  • Don’t lose track of the ladder’s direction. “Nearer the engine” means a lower compartment number; “earlier” means an earlier clock time.
  • Don’t treat a gap of 1 like a plain “earlier than.” Both remove one rung from each end, but a gap of 1 also dies when a third person takes the neighboring rung. On a four-rung ladder, if someone else holds rung 2, the windows (1,2) and (2,3) are gone and the pair must sit on (3,4).

Troubleshooting Common Issues

You reversed “earlier” and “later.” You read “A arrived earlier than B” and crossed out A’s earliest time. Run the extreme test out loud: “If A was earlier than B, could A have arrived dead last at 9:45? No. Nobody came after 9:45.” Cross out 9:45 for A.

A gap clue seems to allow more windows than the table says. You’re probably counting the gap from 0 instead of in steps. “Half an hour before” on a 15-minute ladder is a gap of 2 rungs, not 30 of anything. Convert to rungs first.

You placed the endpoint ✕s and the clue stopped helping. Check the clue again every time either item’s row loses its first or last open square. Ordered clues pay out in installments.

Conclusion

Ordered clues look like they need a placed item before you can use them. They don’t:

  1. Every “earlier than” clue gives up to three ✕s at once: A off the last rung, B off the first rung, and A ≠ B across categories.
  2. Every gap-of-k clue takes k rungs off each end and leaves exactly n − k windows.
  3. Ordered clues stay live. Each new ✕ at the edge of one item’s range can push the other item’s range inward.
  4. Block one end of a window with another clue and the window often collapses to a single forced ●.

Test the compartment ladder yourself in Death on the Night Mail, then take on the act-and-floor bridge in Curtain Call at the Gaslight Theatre.
